## 🔬 What is "Nano Law"?

**"Nano law"** (or "nano-regulation") refers to **legal frameworks specifically designed to regulate nanotechnology** — materials, devices, and systems at the atomic/molecular scale (typically 1-100 nanometers).

It's not one specific law, but rather a **concept/policy domain** covering regulations, guidelines, and governance structures for nanotech.

---

## 📜 Key Findings from Research

### 1. **No Dedicated "Nano-Law" Exists Yet**
- As of 2006, research confirmed: *"there is no tailor-made nano-law in any jurisdiction"* (G Van Calster, European Energy and Environmental Law Review)
- A 2005 Swiss report concluded that a moratorium or specific "Nano-Law" did not seem appropriate at that time

### 2. **Debate: New Laws vs. Existing Frameworks**
Two schools of thought:
- **Adapt existing laws** — modify chemical, food, pharmaceutical regulations to include nanotechnology
- **Create new nano-specific laws** — dedicated legislation for nanotech

### 3. **Regulatory Challenges**
- **"Novelty condition"** — nanotechnology's newness makes regulation difficult (CJ Preston & MY Sheinin, 2010)
- **Risk assessment** — nanoparticles behave differently than bulk materials
- **Unknown long-term effects** — health, environmental impacts not fully understood

### 4. **Major Areas of Nano-Regulation**
- **Food & Agriculture** — EU has been debating nano-food regulations since ~2016
- **Environmental protection** — nanoparticle waste, contamination
- **Consumer products** — cosmetics, textiles, electronics
- **Medicines & health** — drug delivery systems, implants

### 5. **Global Policy Approaches**
- **EU** — tends toward precautionary approach, dedicated nano strategies
- **US** — more industry-friendly, adapts existing frameworks (EPA, FDA)
- **International** — ISO has nanotech standards, but no binding global treaty
